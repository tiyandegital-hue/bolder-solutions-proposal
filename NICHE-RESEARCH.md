## Sources studied (2026-11-03)
- CyberOptik, "20 Best Heavy Equipment Website Designs" (Caterpillar, Heavy
  Haulers): CAT's yellow-and-black identity language reads as "tough" instantly;
  photo sliders of machines in action prove capacity; CTAs prominent; catalogs
  navigable by machine type. Trust = customer testimonials, case studies, finished-work photos.
- ColorWhistle, "Heavy Machinery Website Design Inspiration" + "Heavy Equipment
  Website Design Trends": minimal industrial aesthetics (rugged yet refined);
  interactive catalogs/spec tables; local dealer integration; short clips of
  equipment in action as ambient engagement.
- ACS Web Design, heavy-equipment dealer case study (Mid Country Machinery):
  distinct pages for sales, rental, parts and REPAIR; browsable inventory with
  per-machine detail; inventory credibility = the site proves the machines exist.
- keyideasinfotech, "Construction Equipment and Services Website Design"
  (excavation companies): dedicated service pages per task (earthmoving, site
  prep, grading, trenching, demolition); FLEET CAPABILITY page with project size
  ranges; emergency excavation contact; "never shows a machine" is the anti-pattern
  — buyers want to see the iron.

## Patterns adopted
1. **CAT language, not generic dark-tech**: near-black charcoal + safety amber
   accent is the industry's own colour grammar (quarried from CAT/industrial
   safety codes, not invented). Amber reserved for CTA, eyebrows, hazard-stripe
   dividers — 60-30-10 discipline.
2. **The iron is the proof**: fleet page lists the four REAL machines from the
   directory with their real capabilities — no sample inventory, no invented
   machines. One live signature per page (ghost type, hazard stripes).
3. **Service pages by task, not by machine**: earthmoving/site prep, mechanical
   services, hauling/mobilization, seasonal maintenance — matches how Dawson
   customers actually hire ("I need a driveway graded", not "I need an SVL75").
4. **Local + frontier trust**: Dawson City/Klondike positioning, tap-to-call in
   header and hero (thumb-first on mobile), General Delivery address stated
   honestly (remote-town credibility, not a weakness).
5. **Quote-first conversion**: every page ends at "Request a quote" — phone number
   always visible. No checkout, no fake pricing.
6. **Zero third-party assets**: system fonts (condensed industrial stacks) + inline
   SVG machine line-art — no copyrighted photos, fast, offline-safe.

## Patterns rejected (and why)
- Testimonials, "X years in business", "best in the Yukon", fleet-size numbers,
  licence/certification claims: nothing in the directory supports them. Excluded.
- Stock photos of excavators: licensing + generic look. Replaced with original
  inline-SVG machine line-art drawn for this site.
- Fake project gallery / before-after photos: invented proof kills the trust this
  site exists to build. Fleet facts are the gallery.
- Google Maps embed: would disclose third-party tracking and needs no consent
  flow; replaced with a styled CSS "service area" panel.
